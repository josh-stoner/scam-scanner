# Research Brief: Health Fraud Product Database — Verified Entries for Scam Scanner Seed Data
Date: 2026-03-12 | Sources: 22 | Confidence: High (government enforcement records)

---

## Key Findings

- FDA has identified 1,000+ fraudulent/tainted products; the official searchable database is at https://www.fda.gov/consumers/health-fraud-scams/health-fraud-product-database
- Three dominant taint categories: hidden PDE-5 inhibitors (sildenafil/tadalafil) in sexual enhancement products; hidden controlled substances (sibutramine) in weight loss products; unsubstantiated disease-treatment claims in supplements
- FTC has pursued 10+ major enforcement actions 2020–2026, many with injunctions banning specific claims
- TINA.org filed complaints against 100+ menopause supplement brands in 2024 alone; has active investigations into Goop, Modere, USANA, Nutrafol, Herbalife, Balance of Nature
- BBB tracks supplement subscription traps as an emerging health fraud vector (2024 risk report)

## Gaps / Unknowns

- FDA's full tainted products database has 1,000+ entries; only publicly notified products appear in search results — the bulk require direct database query at https://www.fda.gov/consumers/health-fraud-scams/health-fraud-product-database
- Exact dates for many FDA public notifications are not surfaced in search results; product pages should be scraped individually for precise dates
- BBB Scam Tracker does not surface individual health product names at scale via search — it is a crowdsourced consumer report system, not a structured enforcement database
- "Rhino Gold," "Bull Power," and similar Rhino-branded products are widely flagged but exact FDA notification URLs require direct database lookup
- Instagram/TikTok banned health ad lists are not publicly published by the platforms — no accessible structured list found
- Amazon's own banned product list is not public
- FTC's 2023 notice to 700 supplement companies was a warning, not enforcement — specific company names from that batch are not public
- TINA.org's menopause supplement sweep covered 100 companies; only 2 (Pharmavite/Equelle and Alliance/Amberen) were specifically named in press releases
